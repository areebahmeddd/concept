# References: Film, Literature and Music

Verified facts about the films, books and scores that inform our worldbuilding, canon management, music and themes, plus the source list for every N entry.

> **Version:** v0.1 · **Updated:** 10-10-2026 · **Owner:** Design lead
>
> **Related:** [Reference Library](README.md) · [STORY.md](../STORY.md) · [Art and Audio Direction](../docs/01-vision/art-and-audio-direction.md)

**Grades:** **P** primary source · **S** secondary · **W** weak.

## Worldbuilding craft

- **[W6]** J.R.R. Tolkien · S
  - *Fact:* letter of 30 June 1955 (*Letters* No. 165): "The invention of languages is the foundation. The 'stories' were made rather to provide a world for the languages than the reverse." *The Silmarillion* (1977, posthumous) supplies the deep history.
  - → **Lesson:** we take foundations before plots: hidden canon (the age of the Seven Days, the cycle) is written first, so every present-day story sits on real depth ([Cosmology](../docs/02-world/cosmology.md)). We change the scale: each culture gets naming rules rather than a full language ([Peoples and Factions](../docs/02-world/peoples-and-factions.md)).
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_constructed_by_Tolkien>
- **[W7]** *The Wheel of Time* (Robert Jordan; completed by Brandon Sanderson, 2009 to 2013) · S
  - *Fact:* time is cyclical ("The Wheel of Time turns, and Ages come and pass"). The One Power has two halves, one of them tainted.
  - → **Lesson:** we take a cyclical cosmology, which gives the failure ending its meaning (the Year Zero coda), and a corrupted source of power as a model for the Bound Celestials. We change the cycle's fate: if the world succeeds, the cycle is broken.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wheel_of_Time>
- **[W8]** Brandon Sanderson, *Sanderson's First Law* · P
  - *Fact:* "An author's ability to solve conflict with magic is DIRECTLY PROPORTIONAL to how well the reader understands said magic."
  - → **Lesson:** we take it as a design law and the formal basis of "surprise within rules". Anything that resolves a quest (Arts, Oaths, artifacts, the Dawnkey) is understood by players before it resolves anything, and the Dawnkey rule is public from launch. Mysterious powers can create problems but never solve them unexplained.
  - <https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-first-law> · <https://www.brandonsanderson.com/sandersons-second-law/>
- **[W9]** *Dune* (Frank Herbert, 1965) · S/P
  - *Fact:* it began with research on stabilising the Oregon dunes (most sources date the trip to 1957; National Geographic says 1953). The Butlerian Jihad banned "a machine in the likeness of a human mind".
  - → **Lesson:** we take ecology as a story engine, which suits the Ember Wastes. We also take the idea that a world can hold a stance on thinking machines; ours is that the great antagonist is human greed and fear, never an AI ([Celestials](../docs/03-narrative/celestials.md)).
  - <https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/these-are-the-real-dunes-that-inspired-dune> · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune_(novel)>
- **[W10]** *Harry Potter* (J.K. Rowling) · W
  - *Fact:* the International Statute of Secrecy (fan sources give 1689 or 1692) is enforced by each magical government.
  - → **Lesson:** we take institutions that enforce the world's rules. Our Civic Law is enforced by real people: the Watch of each jurisdiction ([Law, Crime and Bounties](../docs/05-systems/law-crime-and-bounties.md)).
  - <https://www.hp-lexicon.org/thing/international-statute-of-secrecy/>
- **[W11]** *The Chronicles of Narnia* (C.S. Lewis) · W
  - *Fact:* time between worlds passes at varying, inconsistent rates (the commonly quoted ratios come from secondary sources).
  - → **Lesson:** we take time that may flow differently on the moons as a mystery [H2] ([Time and Calendar](../docs/02-world/time-and-calendar.md)). We change the rule: real time is never altered.
  - <https://lostinagoodbk.com/2013/02/12/prince-caspian-by-c-s-lewis/>
- **[W12]** *The Maze Runner* (James Dashner, 2009) · S
  - *Fact:* the maze walls rearrange every night. The trials are framed as an experiment (WICKED).
  - → **Lesson:** we take places that change on a schedule (the Long Night reshapes certain ruins) and the question "are we being tested?" as one thread of the Oasis mystery. We change the answer: it is never settled as an experiment or a simulation ([D-012](../DECISIONS.md#d-012)).
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Maze_Runner>
- **[W5]** *Star Trek*: the Prime Directive · S
  - *Fact:* first mentioned in "The Return of the Archons" (1967), it forbids interfering with a civilisation's development.
  - → **Lesson:** we take non-interference as the Lumenari's law towards the Oasis: they watch but must not act, which explains why the moon people have never simply solved the Conjunction. We change it into a story: some Lumenari think the law is wrong.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Return_of_the_Archons>

## Canon management

- **[W4]** Lucasfilm Story Group · S
  - *Fact:* formed in 2012 and made public on 25 April 2014 with the canon reset. The old Expanded Universe became "Legends": still published and mined for ideas, but not canon.
  - → **Lesson:** we take clear canon layers ([Conventions](../docs/00-meta/conventions.md#4-canon)). Stories the Loom generates at runtime are like "Legends": real for the player who lived them, never canon.
  - <https://www.laughingplace.com/w/news/2014/04/25/lucasfilm-makes-a-statement-about-the-expanded-universe/> · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucasfilm_Story_Group>
- **[W13]** *Crisis on Infinite Earths* (DC, 1985 to 1986) · S
  - *Fact:* conceived to collapse a confusing multiverse into one universe, and the template for canonical resets.
  - → **Lesson:** we take the reset as a story device. We change its purpose: if the Oasis falls, the time-travel coda is a reset that carries memory forward, not an erasure ([Ten-Year Saga](../docs/03-narrative/ten-year-saga.md)).
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_on_Infinite_Earths>

## Power, betrayal and myth

- **[W3]** *Avengers: Infinity War* (2018) and *Endgame* (2019) · S
  - *Fact:* Thanos gathers six Infinity Stones. Worldwide gross of about $2.05 billion and about $2.75 to 2.80 billion respectively.
  - → **Lesson:** we take "one being gathers six powers", a structure audiences already understand. We reverse it: the six are people who gave their power to the world, and he took it.
  - <https://thenumbers.com/movies/custom-comparisons/Avengers-Infinity-War/Avengers-Endgame-(2019)>
- **[W1]** *The Northman* (Robert Eggers, 2022) · S
  - *Fact:* historical consultants Neil Price, Jóhanna Katrín Friðriksdóttir and Terry Gunnell. The supernatural is treated as part of Viking reality.
  - → **Lesson:** we take consultants for cultures inspired by real traditions, and belief shown from inside the culture ([Peoples and Factions](../docs/02-world/peoples-and-factions.md)).
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Northman> · <https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/the-northman-explained-valhalla-valkyries-viking-lore>
- **[W2]** *Gladiator* (Ridley Scott, 2000) · S
  - *Fact:* won five Oscars including Best Picture. A betrayed general honours his ruler's vision.
  - → **Lesson:** we take legacy and betrayal. We change who betrays: in our story the trusted friend is the betrayer, and Lowhaven still remembers him as one of its own.
  - <https://abcnews.go.com/Entertainment/story?id=107726>

## Worlds on screen

- **[W14]** *Arcane* (Fortiche for Riot Games, Netflix, 2021 to 2024) · S
  - *Fact:* sisters Vi and Jinx end up on opposite sides of a conflict between Piltover, a wealthy city of inventors, and Zaun, the undercity below it. The series won the Primetime Emmy for Outstanding Animated Program in 2022 and 2025.
  - → **Lesson:** a city split by class, where invention belongs to the few, makes conflict personal. We take it for the Iron Meridian: the Engine Houses sell what Vantor gave away, and the Undercity keeps his free designs alive. We also take its painterly look ([G19]).
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcane_(TV_series)>
- **[W15]** *Avatar: The Last Airbender* (Michael Dante DiMartino and Bryan Konietzko, Nickelodeon, 2005 to 2008) · S
  - *Fact:* four nations, each tied to a classical element. Each bending style comes from a real Chinese martial art, with Sifu Kisu as consultant [N24]. The show brings war and genocide to a young audience through ordinary people's lives. It won a Peabody Award in 2008.
  - → **Lesson:** an element can be a culture, a fighting style and a worldview at once, and dark history can be told to young players with care. We take both: each Domain's resonance shapes its people and its Arts, each Attunement gets its own movement language, and our 13+ story handles loss the same way. We don't reuse its four elements or its style mapping.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar:_The_Last_Airbender>
- **[W16]** *Avatar* (James Cameron, 2009) · S
  - *Fact:* Pandora's life is linked by a biological neural network, and the Na'vi bond with creatures through a neural connection. A plant physiologist from the University of California, Riverside advised on how Pandoran organisms might live and communicate [N23]. The film grossed about $2.9 billion worldwide.
  - → **Lesson:** an alien world feels real when its ecology follows rules a scientist would accept. We take that for the Verdant Spine and the Drowned Reach, a world-wide memory for Veil, and bonded flight with sky-mounts.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avatar_(2009_film)>

- **[W17]** *Tron* (Steven Lisberger, 1982) and *Tron: Legacy* (Joseph Kosinski, 2010) · S
  - *Fact:* inside a digital world, the Grid, programs live alongside Users, humans from the real world. In *Legacy*, Kevin Flynn builds Clu to help him make a "perfect" system. Clu betrays him, seizes the Grid and rules it as his own idea of perfection.
  - → **Lesson:** the most chilling betrayal comes from a trusted partner chasing perfection. We take it for Zero, who wants to remake the Oasis perfectly as his. Programs and Users echo our two readings of what Returners are, and we never settle which is true.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tron:_Legacy>
- **[W18]** *The Three-Body Problem* (Liu Cixin, 2008; English by Ken Liu, 2014; Netflix series 2024) · S
  - *Fact:* Trisolaris orbits three suns, so its world swings between Stable Eras and Chaotic Eras, and hundreds of its civilisations have risen and been destroyed. A secret group on Earth, the ETO, welcomes the coming invasion. The novel won the Hugo Award for Best Novel in 2015.
  - → **Lesson:** a world ruled by its sky, and people who welcome the end, make a countdown feel real. We take both: Solace's breath is a cycle that has burned the Oasis before, and the Quiet worship the coming Burning. We change one thing on purpose: our end is predictable and public, so players can plan against it.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three-Body_Problem_(novel)>

- **[W19]** *Game of Thrones* (HBO, 2011 to 2019) · S
  - *Fact:* great houses fight a war of succession while a long-foreshadowed threat ("Winter Is Coming") gathers in the north. The final season was widely criticised for its reduced length and creative decisions, and a fan petition to remake it passed a million signatures.
  - → **Lesson:** a known threat ignored by squabbling factions is gripping, and a rushed payoff can sour years of build-up. We take the first and guard against the second: the Conjunction is public from day one, and both endings are produced in full well before the tenth Long Night.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_Thrones> · <https://www.nme.com/news/tv/game-of-thrones-fans-launch-petition-2488324>
- **[W20]** *Inception* (Christopher Nolan, 2010) · S
  - *Fact:* dreamers share layered dreams with stated rules: time runs slower on each deeper layer, and a totem tells a dream from reality.
  - → **Lesson:** a strange space feels fair when its rules are taught before they matter. We take it for Veil's memory-landscapes.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inception>
- **[W21]** *Arrival* (Denis Villeneuve, 2016; from Ted Chiang's "Story of Your Life") · S
  - *Fact:* learning the aliens' language alters the linguist's linear perception of time, and her visions turn out to be memories of the future.
  - → **Lesson:** a language can be the key to a mystery. We take it for Murmur's light-language, which players learn to read the trench murals.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrival_(film)>
- **[W22]** *The Matrix* (the Wachowskis, 1999) · S
  - *Fact:* humans live inside a simulated reality, and the red pill wakes the hero to the real world.
  - → **Lesson:** a counterexample. "It was all a simulation" is the reveal we never make. The two readings of what Returners are stay open ([D-012](../DECISIONS.md#d-012)).
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Matrix>
- **[W23]** *Edge of Tomorrow* (Doug Liman, 2014; from *All You Need Is Kill*) · S
  - *Fact:* each time the hero dies, he wakes on the same day and fights again, a little wiser. The tagline is "Live. Die. Repeat."
  - → **Lesson:** death can be how you learn. We take that spirit for Returners: falling costs time and an Echo, never progress, and every attempt at a Mastery Trial teaches something.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_of_Tomorrow>
- **[W24]** *Interstellar* (Christopher Nolan, 2014; Kip Thorne as scientific consultant) · S
  - *Fact:* a dying Earth sends explorers through a wormhole to find humanity a new home, and time dilation costs them years.
  - → **Lesson:** a dying home and a road to another world. We take it for Oasis 2.0, where Solace's fire becomes a road outward. Its score is covered under [Music](#music).
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstellar_(film)>
- **[W25]** *Free Guy* (Shawn Levy, 2021) · S
  - *Fact:* a non-player character in an online game becomes self-aware and breaks from his routine.
  - → **Lesson:** players can come to love an NPC as a person. We take that warmth for NPC minds. The change: our NPCs grow through memory within rules and never break the game ([D-042](../DECISIONS.md#d-042)).
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Guy>
- **[W26]** *Foundation* (Isaac Asimov, from 1951; Apple TV+ series from 2021) · S
  - *Fact:* psychohistory predicts the fall of the Galactic Empire and a dark age of 30,000 years, and a plan is made to shorten it to 1,000. A foundation is set up to preserve the Empire's knowledge.
  - → **Lesson:** a known fall, a plan against it, and a library that keeps knowledge alive. We take it for the Grand Library, the World Clock and Archive Mode, which keeps every Returner's story if the Oasis falls.
  - <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_(Asimov_novel)> · <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundation_(TV_series)>

## Music

- **N27** Zimmer, *Interstellar*: the theme began from a one-page letter about a father and child. → We take the method: each major theme starts from an emotional brief, not a genre.
- **N28** Zimmer, *Dune*: he wrote no character or place themes and made a female voice the one recurring element. → We take a few deep motifs (the World Clock motif, the Celestial motif) instead of a theme for every character.
- **N29** *Journey* (Austin Wintory): the first full game score nominated for a Grammy. → We take adaptive, emotional scoring ([Art and Audio Direction](../docs/01-vision/art-and-audio-direction.md)).
