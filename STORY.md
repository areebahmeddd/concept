# The Story of the Oasis

The whole saga in one place: the world players step into, the ten years that follow, and the truth underneath. Every world, narrative and quest document grows from this one.

Parts I and II are what players are told and can discover. **Part III is hidden canon**: the truth designers build towards, which players learn only through the [reveal graph](docs/03-narrative/reveal-graph.md). Part IV names what the story is about.

## Prologue: the Herald's Warning

*Launch day. Every Returner on every platform sees it at the same moment, in the same sky.*

The sun Solace hangs over Halcyon as it always has: warm, gold, patient. Then the five Wanderers, the far planets nobody looks at, light up in a line across the sky, like beads on a thread.

A voice speaks from the Grand Library's bell tower. It isn't a god's voice. It is old, tired and very human: the **Herald**, last of the Library's Keepers.

> "Ten times the three moons will meet. On the tenth meeting, the Wanderers will stand in their line, and Solace will breathe. Its breath will burn the Oasis to glass.
>
> It happened once before. We have the records. We know.
>
> There were seven who could have stopped it. Seven who climbed higher than any of us. One of them betrayed the rest. The six are chained in the far places of the world, and the seventh has never shown his face.
>
> Only their power, freely given, can turn Solace's breath aside. Free them. Find him. You have ten years.
>
> You are Returners. You come back when you fall. Nobody knows why. Perhaps this is why."

A great clock face of light appears above the Library. It reads **10 Years · 0 Days**, and it counts down in real time, in public, until the end.

## Part I: the world Returners find

### The Oasis

The Oasis is a world of continents and oceans, mountains and storm-skies, circling the sun **Solace**. Three moons cross its sky:

- **Lumen** is silver and close. Its people, the **Lumenari**, trade with the Oasis by **Skyferry** and keep their own old secrets.
- **Veil** is wrapped in pale mist. Nobody who has gone there has come back to describe it.
- **Hollow** is dark, with a faint red rim. Sailors won't look at it. Children are told not to point.

Once a year the three moons meet and the **Long Night** falls: a full day of darkness. Monsters surge, shops shutter, and doors that stay closed all year swing open. People light lanterns and wait for dawn. Returners go out into the dark, because that is when the world shows its secrets.

### The Returners

Most people live one life. **Returners** come back. When one falls, they wake at a **Wayshrine**, shaken but alive. They arrive as adults with no childhood anyone remembers, from somewhere nobody can name. People welcome them, fear them, hire them and gossip about them. The old say Returners were rare once. Now they arrive in their thousands, as if the world had called them for something.

The player is a Returner: one avatar, one life story, many deaths ([D-019](DECISIONS.md#d-019)).

### The Domains

Six great regions surround the central **Hearthlands**. Each feels like a world of its own, because in a sense it is: the power of the Celestial chained inside it sets its **Natural Law**, what works there and what fails ([World Laws](docs/02-world/world-laws.md)).

| Domain | What Returners find | Its law, in a sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Verdant Spine** | Mountains older than memory, forests tall enough to have their own weather, mountain clans and wandering shrines | *The forest rejects what doesn't belong.* Large machines falter; roots remember paths. |
| **The Drowned Reach** | Three cities under the sea: **Pelagia** of the coral spires, **Murmur** in the trenches, whose people speak in light, and **Saltglass**, a domed city at the edge of the deep | *Fire sleeps under water.* Pressure is a wall only preparation can climb. |
| **The Stormcrown** | Islands floating in a storm that never ends, sky-nomads, lightning harvesters | *The sky is lighter here.* Flight is easier, and falls are longer. |
| **The Ember Wastes** | Deserts of glass where something burned the sand long ago, caravan kingdoms, oases guarded like treasure | *Noon is a weapon.* At midday, flight burns and metal sings with heat. |
| **The Iron Meridian** | A megacity of gears, rails and neon, piloted Frames in the streets, ancient Wardens asleep in the foundations | *Machines are welcome; wild magic is not.* Arts stutter and engineering sings. |
| **The Frostmarch** | Ice fields under auroras, old kingdoms, monasteries that keep time by star-wheels | *Time is thick here.* Memories linger in the air. |

In the Hearthlands lie **Halcyon**, the capital, where every Returner first arrives and the **Grand Library** keeps the world's memory; **Lowhaven**, a fishing town on the grey coast; and **Sennight**, a ruined city nobody rebuilds.

### The legend of the Seven, as people tell it

Ask in a tavern and you'll hear it in pieces. Nobody tells it whole.

- *"There were Returners, long ago, who didn't just come back. They went **up**. They kept a promise so long and so hard that it changed them. Celestials, the old books call them."*
- *"There were seven of them. Or six. My grandmother always said six, and then 'the other one'."*
- *"One of them wanted it all. He fought the rest for seven days and seven nights at Everbright, the brightest city in the world. That's why we call it Sennight now. Nothing grows there."*
- *"He won. Nobody knows how. He took their power and they went away, to the edges. My uncle saw something walking on the Verdant Spine that was taller than the mountains."*
- *"He's still out there, the false one. Never seen. My mother says he lived in Lowhaven once. Ordinary man. Mended nets."*
- *"The Library has the records. The old Keepers wrote everything down. You just have to know how to read them."*

### The countdown

The **World Clock** over Halcyon counts down to the **Great Conjunction**. Every Long Night, the Wanderers stand a little closer to their line. Every year, Solace burns a little hotter at noon, and the old glass of the Ember Wastes shows what that heat once did.

The task is simple to say and enormous to do: **free the six, find the seventh, forge the Dawnkey, and turn Solace's breath aside.** Nobody can do it alone. Everyone can help.

## Part II: the ten years

The decade unfolds in Ages, each built around a **Convergence**: a world-wide effort to free one Celestial ([Celestial Convergence](docs/05-systems/celestial-convergence.md)). The [Ten-Year Saga](docs/03-narrative/ten-year-saga.md) sets out each Age, and the [Ten-Year Live Plan](docs/11-production/ten-year-live-plan.md) sets out the dates.

- **The Arrival.** Returners spread across the Oasis, find the Library's first records and walk Sennight's ruins. On the first Long Night the mountains of the Verdant Spine are seen to move.
- **The Age of Roots.** The world climbs a living mountain and frees **Orun**. His release proves the six were chained, not dead, and that each one freed gives their Oath to everyone.
- **The middle Ages.** The community chooses the order in which **Maris**, **Kestra**, **Sahr** and **Vantor** are freed. In the drowned cities Returners learn that some trials are won by understanding, not force. The sky turns to war, and the first private ships rise towards the moons. In the glass deserts the truth about the last Burning surfaces, along with the real price of the Dawnkey. The mist of **Veil** parts. Colossal Frames walk and the old Wardens wake.
- **The Age of Hours.** Time itself becomes the battlefield. Freeing **Ysolde** opens the Library's deepest vault, and the false one's name with it.
- **The Hollow Age.** The dark moon opens. The world confronts the **False Celestial**. Then comes the **Dawn Rite** on the tenth Long Night.

### The two endings

- **If the Dawnkey is forged in time,** every Returner who swore the seventh Oath stands under the aligned moons. Solace breathes, and its fire turns outward past the Wanderers, lighting a road into the dark. The Oasis endures, and **Oasis 2.0** begins along that road, towards other suns and other worlds.
- **If it isn't,** Solace breathes and the world burns to glass as the clock reaches zero. The live world ends at exactly ten real years, as promised. In its last moments the Grand Library does what it was built for: it remembers. The final scene shows a shoreline, a fishing town, a child mending nets, and a clock reading **Year Zero**. The cycle turns. **Archive Mode** keeps every Returner's story ([D-047](DECISIONS.md#d-047)).

## Part III: hidden canon

Designers only. In the game, each truth below arrives through the [reveal graph](docs/03-narrative/reveal-graph.md), after its foreshadowing.

### 1. The Oasis has burned before

The Great Conjunction is a cycle. The last time it came, the Oasis burned, and the glass of the Ember Wastes is what remains. The world was remade afterwards. The Lumenari watched it happen. Their oldest law, the Stillness, forbids them to interfere in the Oasis's fate, and they have carried that guilt for an age. Some of them now think the law is wrong ([Peoples and Factions](docs/02-world/peoples-and-factions.md)).

### 2. How the world was remade, and what Returners are

The world was remade from memory. The mist of **Veil** is the Oasis's memory: everything that ever lived there leaves an impression in it. After the last Burning, the world was rebuilt from those impressions. That is why the Oasis feels both ancient and new, and why its ruins are older than the cultures living among them.

**Returners** are people who are remembered back. When they fall, Veil remembers them and they wake at a Wayshrine, which is one of Veil's anchors on the Oasis. Where they come from before their first arrival is the deepest question of the saga, and the world gives two answers that are never collapsed into one: *they are travellers from somewhere beyond the Oasis*, and *they are the world dreaming its own heroes*. Players, who know they are people at a screen entering a made world, will hear the first. The characters hold the second. Both are true in the fiction ([D-012](DECISIONS.md#d-012)).

### 3. Ascension: the first six

In the age before the last Burning, the Age of Everbright, a handful of Returners did something nobody had done. Each swore an **Oath** in service of others and kept it for a lifetime, through death after death. Each Oath was kept so deeply that it became a law of the world, and its keeper became a **Celestial**.

| Celestial | Who they were | Their Oath |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Orun** | A mountain guide who carried the injured over the Spine thousands of times and never left anyone behind | *"I will not stop while someone still needs carrying."* (Perseverance) |
| **Maris** | A diver of the drowned cities who sang for the drowned so their families could grieve | *"No one will be forgotten by the sea."* (Compassion) |
| **Kestra** | A sky-courier who flew into storms nobody else would fly into, to reach the stranded | *"I will go where others cannot."* (Courage) |
| **Sahr** | A lamp-keeper of the desert who walked into a solar flare to shield a caravan, and walked out holding it | *"I will carry the burning so others need not."* (Sacrifice) |
| **Vantor** | An engineer who built machines for anyone and gave every design away | *"What I make, I make for all hands."* (Ingenuity) |
| **Ysolde** | A scribe who founded the Grand Library so that no one's life would vanish | *"I will keep every hour."* (Memory) |

When the last Conjunction came near, the six could have stopped it. Their combined Oaths could form the **Dawnkey**, but they were one short. The Dawnkey needs seven Oaths, freely given.

### 4. The seventh: Player Zero

The seventh was the first.

He was the **first Returner of that age**, the first person Veil ever remembered back. The oldest records call him only by the mark the Library gave him: **Zero**. He grew up mending nets in **Lowhaven**. He was kind, funny and endlessly brave. He taught the others how to return. He was Orun's friend, Maris's student and Kestra's rival. The six trusted him more than anyone alive, and when they needed a seventh Oath, everyone assumed it would be his.

But Zero had seen something the others hadn't. Diving into Veil's memory, he had watched the last Burning, and he understood that the Dawnkey only turns Solace's breath aside. It doesn't end the cycle. The Conjunction will come again, and again. Something in him broke. If the cycle can't be ended, he reasoned, only one thing matters: surviving it, and being the one who remakes the world afterwards.

He swore his Oath, *"I will save them all"*, and then he broke it. He went to Everbright, where the six had gathered to forge the Dawnkey. He knew every one of their Oaths, because he had helped them keep them. For **seven days** he fought them, and on the seventh he turned each Oath against its keeper. He made Orun unable to stop. He made Maris forget. He chained each of them with their own promise.

He took their power. Stolen power can't forge a Dawnkey, but it can survive the Burning. Zero withdrew to **Hollow**, the dark moon, and watched the Oasis burn. When the world was remade from Veil's memory, he made sure it was remade with the six still chained, each in a far Domain. Their chained power became the Natural Laws of those regions. **That is why the world has the shape it has.**

He has steered the Oasis ever since, not as a tyrant on a throne but as a hand on the scale: nudging Natural Law, erasing the Library's records, keeping the six asleep. Now the Conjunction comes again, and he means to let it burn the world once more and remake the next Oasis himself, perfectly, as his. He calls it mercy. That is what makes him frightening: he believes he alone understands the stakes. He is right about the cycle and wrong about everything else.

He is "the False Celestial" because his ascension was a theft. He never kept an Oath. He broke one.

### 5. Inside Hollow

Hollow is hollow. Inside it, Zero rebuilt **Everbright** as it was on the morning before the Seven Days, perfect and silent, and he lives there alone among statues of his six friends. Players see it only in the Hollow Age, and it is the stage of the final Convergence ([Celestials](docs/03-narrative/celestials.md)).

### 6. The Herald

The Herald is what remains of **Ysolde's** Oath outside her chains. When Zero chained her in the Frostmarch, a sliver of her promise to keep every hour escaped into the Library she had founded, and it has kept the records ever since, in the shape of an old Keeper who doesn't remember being anyone else. When Ysolde is freed, the Herald understands, says goodbye, and fades into her.

### 7. Why the six must be fought

The six aren't villains. They are **Bound**: chained by their own Oaths, turned inside out. Orun can't stop walking. Maris drowns everyone in grief because she can't remember why she sings. Each Convergence frees a Celestial by breaking the chain, and the way to break each chain is written in that Celestial's Oath. A freed Celestial, restored for a few moments, gives their Oath to every Returner. Each one strengthens the world and adds a piece to the Dawnkey.

### 8. The Dawnkey and the seventh Oath

Six freed Oaths aren't enough. They weren't enough before, either. The seventh can't be taken from Zero, because his was broken. **It has to be given by the Returners themselves.** At the **Dawn Rite** on the tenth Long Night, every Returner who chooses to swears the **Returners' Oath**. Its words stay hidden until that night. Its meaning is *"We will carry each other."*

The final Convergence is fought to take back the six stolen Oaths, and to face Zero's question: if the cycle can never end, is the world still worth saving? The Returners' answer is their Oath. How the world fights him decides his fate ([D-017](DECISIONS.md#d-017)).

### 9. The endings, in truth

- **Success.** The Dawnkey does more than turn Solace's breath aside. Because the seventh Oath was given by thousands rather than one, its power is greater than the six ever imagined, and it **breaks the cycle**. Solace's fire becomes a road outward. Along it lies a second world circling a cold blue star, whose own Veil remembers Returners who have never been to the Oasis. That world is where **Oasis 2.0** begins.
- **Failure.** The world burns, as Zero intended. But the Grand Library remembers everything, including every Returner who tried. The time-travel coda is the Library sending that memory into the next cycle's Veil. Year Zero. A child in Lowhaven mending nets, and this time the memory of thousands of Returners goes with him. Whether this Zero keeps his Oath is the question the story ends on.

## Part IV: what the story is about

| Theme | How it shows up |
| --- | --- |
| **Power given, not taken** | The Celestials rose by keeping Oaths for others. Zero took. The Dawnkey can only be forged from what is given. Even the business model follows it: power can't be bought. |
| **Memory and time** | Veil, the Grand Library, Chronicles, Ysolde, and NPCs who grow older over the ten years |
| **Resolve** | The last levels are gruelling by design. Oaths cost something. The Celestials kept their promises through death after death. |
| **Fear dressed as protection** | Zero believes he is saving the world. The most dangerous villains are certain they're right. |
| **Together by choice** | Nobody can do it alone. Everyone can help. The last Oath is "We will carry each other." |

**Where it comes from.** *Ready Player One* gives us a quest that decides a world's fate, and a public scoreboard [R1]. We turn "one winner inherits everything" into "everyone survives together, or nobody does". *Ready Player Two*'s seven shards [R4] remind us that seven pieces only matter if each carries a person's heart. *Dororo* [J7] and *Infinity War* [W3] show the pull of reclaiming stolen power. Berserk's Griffith [J10] is the trusted friend who betrays everyone. *Frieren* [N21] gives us the ache of passing time; *Majora's Mask* and *Outer Wilds* [G17] the falling moon and the dying sun; *The Wheel of Time* [W7] the turning cycle; *Star Trek*'s Prime Directive [W5] the watchers who may not help. And Sanderson's First Law [W8] means every player will understand how the Dawnkey works long before it is used.
