# Celestials

Who the seven Celestials are, what Oath each kept, what their chains made of them, how each is freed, and what their release gives the world.

## 1. Structural decisions

| Question | Decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal or global? | **Global.** Each Celestial is freed once, for everyone ([D-030](../../DECISIONS.md#d-030)). | Pillar P1, One World, One Fate |
| In what order? | **Orun first. Maris, Kestra, Sahr and Vantor in an order the community shapes, with two ready at a time. Ysolde always last of the six, because her release reveals Zero's name. The False Celestial last of all** ([D-016](../../DECISIONS.md#d-016)). | Teaches the pattern, gives players a say, and protects the reveal chain and the finale ([The Ages](ten-year-saga.md#2-the-ages)) |
| Killed, persuaded or something else? | **Freed, not killed.** Each has a different key, written in their Oath. Some phases can be won through understanding. The False Celestial is defeated, and how decides his fate ([VII · The False Celestial](#9-vii--the-false-celestial-player-zero)). | The theme of power given against power taken |
| How does it escalate? | Each Convergence adds a new kind of play: a ground climb, then understanding, the sky, a shared burden, machines, time, and finally all of them at once. | Escalation in kind, not just in numbers |
| What about solo players? | Every phase accepts solo contributions ([D-024](../../DECISIONS.md#d-024)). | Solo play is always viable |
| What about late joiners? | They miss the live event and can relive it as a Chronicle, without its canonical rewards ([D-030](../../DECISIONS.md#d-030)). | Being there matters, and the story stays readable |

**The cliché test.** Each Celestial passes all four:

1. They are a person with a life before ascension.
2. Their Bound form is a tragic inversion of their virtue.
3. Freeing them needs a different kind of play.
4. Their release changes the world, through an era shift and a Gift.

## 2. The pattern every Celestial follows

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Oath** | The vow they kept until it made them a Celestial |
| **Bound form** | What the False Celestial's chain made of them: their Oath turned against them |
| **Chain** | The specific inversion. The key to breaking it lies in the Oath. |
| **Convergence** | Discover, Prepare, Unseal, Confront and Release ([Celestial Convergence](../05-systems/celestial-convergence.md)) |
| **Release** | A brief restored moment in which they speak, remember and give their Oath |
| **Gift** | A Celestial Oath variant granted to every Returner, plus a softening of their Domain's Natural Law (the era shift) |
| **Dawnkey piece** | Their freed Oath becomes one of the seven pieces |

## 3. I · Orun, the Mountain That Walks (Perseverance)

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who he was** | A Cairnfolk mountain guide who carried the injured over the Verdant Spine thousands of times, dying and returning, and never left anyone behind |
| **Oath** | *"I will not stop while someone still needs carrying."* |
| **Bound form** | A mountain-sized colossus of stone and ancient forest, walking the Spine without end. Each step shifts paths and sets off landslides. Villages move their homes every season to stay out of his way. |
| **Chain** | His Oath, inverted. Zero made him believe that everyone still needs carrying, forever, so he can never stop. |
| **Key** | Show him that the people are carried: they can carry each other. |
| **Discover** | Track his path, read Cairnfolk songs and find the six resting stones where he once paused. |
| **Prepare** | Guide villages to safety, build rope-bridges and root-roads, and gather climbing gear. |
| **Unseal** | On the first Long Night (LN1), light all six resting stones. If the world misses LN1, a fallback rite unseals him at the next full phase of Lumen (within 28 rt days) once his Discover and Prepare phases are complete. |
| **Confront** | A climb up the walking colossus. Ground teams climb, flyers distract the forest-heads on his shoulders, and Frames brace his knees on root-roads. This is a declared quest override ([WLD-014](../02-world/world-laws.md#3-the-evaluation-model)): medium Frames are allowed on the Convergence's root-roads during Confront only. His era shift later makes root-road Frames permanent. |
| **Release** | At his heart, the community carries him: every participant's contribution appears as hands holding the stone. Orun stops. He sits, and a mountain range settles for the first time in an age. He says one line, *"Then I can rest,"* and gives his Oath. |
| **Gift** | **Oath of Carrying**, a universal Oath variant: revive and carry allies faster. **Era shift:** the Spine's law softens, and medium Frames may use marked root-roads. |
| **Foreshadowing** | The walking mountains on the first Long Night, Cairnfolk lullabies, and a Library page that says "the carrier never sleeps" |
| **Horizon** | H1. Discover and Prepare open at launch. |

## 4. II · Maris, the Tide That Remembers (Compassion)

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who she was** | A diver of the drowned cities who sang for the drowned so their families could grieve |
| **Oath** | *"No one will be forgotten by the sea."* |
| **Bound form** | A living tide, half woman and half storm-surge, drowning the coasts in floods of grief. Her song now makes people forget. |
| **Chain** | Zero made her forget why she sings. |
| **Key** | Understanding, not violence: give her memories back. |
| **Discover** | Collect the Songs of the Drowned across Pelagia, Murmur and Saltglass. In Murmur the songs are patterns of light. |
| **Prepare** | Evacuate and protect coastal towns, and build song-lanterns. |
| **Unseal** | A choir across all three cities, kept in time across layers. |
| **Confront** | Her surges are fought by holding the line against grief-creatures, and every phase can also end through understanding: players bring her remembered names and stories. The more memories returned, the shorter the fighting. |
| **Release** | She remembers, and sings every name back. |
| **Gift** | **Oath of Remembrance:** longer windows to recover an Echo, and deeper NPC grief quests. **Era shift:** fire still sleeps under water, but light Arts now work in the deep. |
| **Lesson for players** | Some Celestial trials are won by compassion. |

## 5. III · Kestra, the Unbound Wind (Courage)

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who she was** | A sky-courier who flew into storms nobody else would fly into, to reach the stranded |
| **Oath** | *"I will go where others cannot."* |
| **Bound form** | The storm itself: a vast serpent of wind and lightning that tears apart anything that tries to follow her |
| **Chain** | She can go where no one else can, so no one can ever reach her. She is utterly alone. |
| **Key** | Reach her. Prove that someone can follow. |
| **Discover** | Chart the paths through the storm and recover the courier letters she never delivered. |
| **Prepare** | Build storm-anchors, train flight and craft lightning-rods. |
| **Unseal** | Deliver her undelivered letters during a Stillsky season, when the storm's Eye opens. Stillsky comes every 52 rt days ([Time and Calendar](../02-world/time-and-calendar.md#4-regional-seasons-driven-by-the-moons)). |
| **Confront** | War in the air. Flyers, sky-mounts and aircraft chase her through the storm's layers while ground teams on the floating isles hold the anchors. |
| **Release** | The first Returner to reach her, with thousands behind, touches her hand. |
| **Gift** | **Oath of the Open Sky:** a flight stamina bonus in storms for everyone. **Era shift:** the Stormcrown's storms calm into seasons of navigable weather. Personal ships arrive by date at the third Long Night, not with Kestra ([Ten-Year Live Plan](../11-production/ten-year-live-plan.md)). |

## 6. IV · Sahr, the Lantern in the Dust (Sacrifice)

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who he was** | A desert lamp-keeper who walked into a solar flare to shield a caravan, and walked out holding it |
| **Oath** | *"I will carry the burning so others need not."* |
| **Bound form** | A walking lantern of solar fire who burns everything near him: the heat he once carried for others, now turned on them |
| **Chain** | He was made to carry all the burning alone, forever, until it consumed him. |
| **Key** | Share the burden. |
| **Discover** | Uncover the history of the last Burning through glass archaeology, and find Sahr's lantern-shrines. |
| **Prepare** | Build heat-wards and organise night caravans. |
| **Unseal** | At noon, the most dangerous hour under the Ember Wastes' Natural Law, many players each take a share of his heat. |
| **Confront** | A shared-burden mechanic: his heat is a pool divided among everyone taking part. More players means less burden each, so the fight rewards bringing people in rather than raw damage. |
| **Release** | The heat spreads across thousands of hands until it can be borne. |
| **Gift** | **Oath of the Shared Flame:** allies in range split damage over time. **Era shift:** noon is less deadly. **Story:** the Library reveals the Dawnkey's true cost: it needs seven given Oaths (RV-04). |

## 7. V · Vantor, the First Engineer (Ingenuity)

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who he was** | A Meridian engineer who built machines for anyone and gave every design away |
| **Oath** | *"What I make, I make for all hands."* |
| **Bound form** | A citadel-machine eating the Iron Meridian's foundries, building endlessly for no one. Under his corrupted command, the Wardens begin to wake. |
| **Chain** | He makes for no hands at all: creation without purpose. |
| **Key** | Build together, and give his creations back to people. |
| **Discover** | Recover Vantor's free designs across the city. Some are held by Engine Houses, which means heists and negotiations. |
| **Prepare** | Build Colossal Frames from the designs as a world-wide crafting effort. |
| **Unseal** | Engineers reroute power across the whole city. |
| **Confront** | A battle at machine scale: Colossal Frames against the Wardens and the citadel, infantry inside the machine sabotaging its core, and flyers on the towers. |
| **Release** | Players hand the rebuilt Wardens back to the city. |
| **Gift** | **Oath of Open Hands:** bonuses when players craft for other players. **Era shift:** Arts misfire less in the Iron Meridian, and the Wardens become protectors against colossi. |

## 8. VI · Ysolde, the Keeper of Hours (Memory)

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who she was** | A northern scribe who founded the Grand Library so that no one's life would vanish |
| **Oath** | *"I will keep every hour."* |
| **Bound form** | A frozen moment: time locked around Hallowmere, where the same hour of the Seven Days repeats forever with her trapped inside it |
| **Chain** | She keeps one hour, the worst one, forever. |
| **Key** | Let time move: complete the record. |
| **Discover** | Explore the Frostmarch's scenes from the past and find the Library's missing pages, which Zero erased. |
| **Prepare** | Every Chronicle Returners have ever recorded strengthens the record, a callback to every earlier Convergence. |
| **Unseal** | Read the missing pages aloud at Hallowmere during Starfall, when the star-wheels align. Starfall comes every 36 rt days. |
| **Confront** | A battle broken across time. Each phase replays moments from earlier Convergences, and the arena depends on how the world actually resolved them. |
| **Release** | Time moves. The Herald, who is what remained of her Oath outside the chains, understands, says goodbye and fades into her (RV-14). Ysolde reads the last page aloud: the False Celestial's name, **Zero**. |
| **Gift** | **Oath of the Kept Hour:** Chronicles give deeper lore, and each Returner gains a personal memory journal. **Era shift:** scenes from the past can be explored everywhere. **Story:** the way to Hollow's heart is revealed. |
| **Order** | Always last of the six. She becomes ready once four Celestials are freed and can unseal only after the fifth ([D-016](../../DECISIONS.md#d-016)). |

## 9. VII · The False Celestial (Player Zero)

| Element | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Who he was** | The first Returner of the Age of Everbright, a net-mender from Lowhaven, and friend and teacher to the six ([The Story of the Oasis](../../STORY.md#4-the-seventh-player-zero)) |
| **Oath (broken)** | *"I will save them all."* |
| **What he became** | The thief of six Oaths, hidden in Hollow, quietly steering the world's laws for an age. He means to let the Conjunction burn the Oasis and remake it as his own. |
| **Motive** | Fear that calls itself mercy. He saw that the Dawnkey only turns the Burning aside, and decided that only survival and control matter. |
| **Presence before Hollow** | Never seen, only felt: Natural Laws flickering, Library pages erased, the whispers of the Quiet, and a crescent mark on a Lowhaven cottage door. At the ninth Long Night he speaks once to every Returner at the same moment. |
| **Discover** | Explore Hollow, which opens by date at the eighth Long Night whatever the state of the world, and piece together his story from Lowhaven, the Library and the memories of the six. |
| **Prepare** | The whole world gets ready. Every freed Gift and Oath matters. |
| **Unseal** | The memories of the six freed Celestials open Hollow's heart, so all six must be freed first. The earliest date is 8.5 years after launch. |
| **Confront** | Inside Hollow, Zero rebuilt Everbright as it was on the morning before the Seven Days, and lives there alone among statues of the six ([The Story of the Oasis](../../STORY.md#5-inside-hollow)). That silent city is the battlefield. He fights with the six stolen Oaths, so each of the six phases echoes an earlier Convergence: climbing, compassion, sky, shared burden, machines and time. Each phase can be broken by force or by understanding. Returners who keep their declared Oaths through the fight add Resolve to the world's effort, so the theme becomes the mechanic. |
| **The Question** | In the final moments he asks every participant, through the NPC Mind system, why a world that will burn again is worth saving. Answers are free-form. No AI judges them, and they never affect the outcome. Every answer is written into the Grand Library's Chronicle of the end. |
| **His fate** | Set by the **Mercy threshold** ([D-017](../../DECISIONS.md#d-017)). If at least 4 of the 6 phases are resolved by understanding rather than force, Zero, faced with thousands who kept their Oaths, gives back what he stole, keeping his Oath at last in its truest form, and fades into Veil, remembered. Otherwise he is unmade and the stolen Oaths are taken back by force. Either way, the Dawnkey can be forged. |
| **Then** | The Dawn Rite at the tenth Long Night ([The endings](ten-year-saga.md#6-the-endings)) |

**The understanding against force record.** From Convergence I onwards, the World Clock board shows a public record of how every Confront phase was broken: mostly by understanding or mostly by force. The board doesn't say what the record is for. Its meaning, that the six phases of Convergence VII decide Zero's fate, is revealed when Convergence VII opens. The tally rules are in [Celestial Convergence](../05-systems/celestial-convergence.md).

**Writing rules for Zero:**

- **CEL-001 [H1]** Zero is human greed and fear, never "an AI" or "a program".
- **CEL-002 [H1]** Zero is right that the cycle is real and wrong in what he concludes from it.
- **CEL-003 [H1]** No player, NPC or AI may see or write his true name before Ysolde's release. This is a hard lock in the [Reveal Graph](reveal-graph.md#3-node-table) (RV-07).

## 10. Inspirations

| Source | What we take | What we change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The demigods of *Elden Ring* [N2] | Great beings bound to regions, each holding a share of power | They are freed, not slain, and they give their power away |
| The Infinity Stones in *Avengers: Infinity War* [W3] | One being gathering six powers | Reversed: the six gave, and he took |
| *Dororo* [J7] | Taking back stolen pieces | The pieces return to the whole world, not to one hero |
| Griffith in *Berserk* [J10] | The beloved friend who betrays everyone | Zero's motive is fear, and the community decides his fate |
| Clu in *Tron: Legacy* [W17] | A trusted partner who betrays his creator to build a "perfect" world | Zero's perfection is a world remade for him alone, and the community decides whether he gives it up |
| *Vinland Saga* [J9] | "You have no enemies": the strength to end a fight without violence | Maris teaches it, and the Mercy threshold makes it count at the end |
| The Twelve Kizuki in *Demon Slayer* [N20] | A ladder of escalating foes with tragic pasts | Our six aren't evil; each rung is a new kind of play |
| *Shadow of the Colossus* [N14] | A colossal boss you climb | You free the colossus instead of killing it |
| The seven shards in *Ready Player Two* [R4] | Seven pieces spread across worlds | Each piece is a person's Oath, not an object, and Zero is never an AI ([CEL-001](#9-vii--the-false-celestial-player-zero)) |
| The Philosopher's Stone in *Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood* [J11] | Power made from other people's lives | Zero's strength is six Oaths stolen from his friends, and freeing them takes it back |
