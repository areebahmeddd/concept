## 1. Machines: a history

| Era | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| **The Age of Everbright** (before the Burning) | Vantor designed machines "for all hands" and gave the designs away. He built the **Wardens**, huge autonomous Automata, to protect settlements from colossi. |
| **The Burning and the remaking** | Most machines were lost. The Wardens sleep under the Iron Meridian. Fragments of Vantor's free designs survive. |
| **Now** | The Engine Houses of the Iron Meridian rebuilt the industry, and they sell what Vantor gave away. Free tinkerers keep the old open designs alive. |

The history carries the story's main theme: power given against power taken. Vantor's machines were gifts. The Engine Houses sell them.

## 2. Machine classes

| Class | Scale | Who controls it | Needs | Legal status | Horizon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Companion Automaton** | Pet to human size | Its owner (it acts on its own and takes commands) | Spark-cells, repair | Legal everywhere except some sacred sites | H1 |
| **Utility Automaton** | Human to cart size | Its owner | Spark-cells, a licence for heavy use | Legal; limited by Natural Law in some Domains | H1 |
| **Frame** | A 4 to 8 m piloted machine | One pilot | Pilot licence, Spark-cells, maintenance, a hangar | Limited by Civic Law in cities and by Natural Law in some Domains ([World Laws](world-laws.md#4-natural-law-by-domain)) | H1 in the Iron Meridian and Convergences; H2 wider ownership |
| **Colossal Frame** | 30 to 60 m, several crew | A crew of 3 to 6 (pilot, gunner, engineer and others) | Guild sponsorship, a hangar-yard | Military grade. Legal only in sanctioned battle zones and Convergences. | H2 |
| **Warden** | 40 to 80 m, ancient and autonomous | No one, until Vantor is freed | None | None | H2, waking in the Age of Engines |

Machines can be damaged and wrecked, but nothing a player owns is lost for good ([D-039](../../DECISIONS.md#d-039)).

**Why Frames don't rule the world.** Machines need physical reasons for how and where they fight:

1. **Resonance interference.** Outside the Iron Meridian, Natural Laws limit them.
2. **Logistics.** Frames need Spark-cells, maintenance, hangars and licences.
3. **Counterplay.** Some Arts and weapons disrupt Spark systems, and some terrain and Domains favour the small.

## 3. Colossi

**What people know.** Giant creatures appear more often as the Conjunction nears, and scholars argue about why.

**Hidden canon.** Colossi are born where the bound Celestials' power overflows. Each Domain's colossi reflect its Celestial's chained Oath, and they grow more common each Long Year as the chains strain. When a Domain's Celestial is freed, its colossi calm down, migrate or become protectors as part of the era shift.

Colossi are grouped on a readable scale of threat:

| Category | Size | Effect on the world | Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I · Roamer | A building | Local danger; travel reroutes | Groups of players |
| II · Ravager | A tower | Threatens a settlement; ecosystems shift | A town defence event |
| III · Titan | A hill | Reshapes terrain; a region-wide alert | A world event with Frames and air support |
| IV · Leviathan | A mountain | Blocks routes for days; triggers migrations | An event across many layers |
| V · Celestial-born | Domain scale | Only the Bound Celestials and their aspects | A Convergence ([Celestial Convergence](../05-systems/celestial-convergence.md)) |

- **WLD-060 [H1]** Colossi are designed ecology first: diet, habitat, migration and effects on other creatures. None is just a large health bar.
- **WLD-061 [H1]** Every colossus can be fought from the ground, from the air and with machines: climbable weak points, airborne targets and Frame-scale grapples.
- **WLD-062 [H1]** Every arrival is signalled at least 3 rt minutes ahead by tremors, fleeing animals, NPC warnings and Weaver alerts, so players can prepare or evacuate.
- **WLD-063 [H1]** Damage to settlements is real and always recoverable. A Ravager can wreck a town's market, and NPCs and players rebuild it in a recovery event. Essential services move for a while but never vanish ([D-031](../../DECISIONS.md#d-031)).
- **WLD-064 [H1]** Every species is original. No designs or terms from *Pacific Rim*, *Godzilla* or other protected works ([Legal and Compliance](../12-legal/compliance.md)).

**Species at launch (H1):**

| Species | Region | Category | Behaviour | What it gives the world |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gravemaw** | Ember Wastes | II–III (a threat) | Burrows towards heat and metal, collapses dunes and eats glass | Glass-cores for crafting; buried ruins exposed |
| **Skyreef Ray** | Stormcrown | IV (peaceful) | Migrates on a seasonal loop. Its back is a floating ecosystem players can visit and live on for one Stormcrown season (13 rt days). | Rare sky-flora; a moving hub |

**Species through the decade (H2):** the Moving Mountains (Orun's aspects, during Convergence I), the Thornback Leviathan (Drowned Reach), the Rime Titan (Frostmarch) and others.

## 4. Inspirations

| Source | What we take | What we change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Mobile Suit Gundam* [J5] | Machines need physical reasons for how they fight | Our reasons are Natural Law and logistics, so the world stays on a human scale outside the Iron Meridian |
| *Pacific Rim* [J6] | A clear category scale for giant threats | Our categories measure effect on the world, and every species is original |
| *Shadow of the Colossus* [N14] | Each colossus designed around how it is overcome | Colossi are part of the ecosystem, and Orun is freed, not killed |
| *Monster Hunter* [M46] and *Horizon* [G6] | Ecology-first creatures with readable anatomy | Colossi change settlements, travel and seasons, not only fights |
| *Pluto* [J13] | Robots whose feelings have consequences | Companion Automata and the Wardens carry loyalty and grief from what they have lived through |
